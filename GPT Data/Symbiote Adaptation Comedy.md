YES. This has a wonderfully strange engine hiding underneath the slime. 😂 What begins as **“astronomy nerd gets eaten by space Jell-O”** can gradually become a comedy about whether humanity is actually being invaded at all.

The central joke is that humanity keeps treating the organism as an enemy, while the organism keeps interpreting every attack as **useful evolutionary feedback**.



## The core premise

I'd sharpen the organism into something that isn't really a Venom-style parasite. That's merely what humanity *thinks* it is.

It's closer to an **adaptive biological operating system**.

It arrives with almost no predetermined terrestrial biology. Instead:

**Encounter → absorb information → experiment → stabilize → reproduce successful adaptation.**

So the protagonist isn't merely Patient Zero.

He's **Template Zero**.

The organism spends hours wrapped around him after the impact because it has never encountered terrestrial multicellular life before. It's learning:

> *Oh. This is what survives here.*

Temperature.

Oxygen.

Blood chemistry.

Bacteria.

Human locomotion.

Digestion.

Waste removal.

Sleep.

Pain.

Language.

Eventually social behavior.

And unfortunately...

**clothing.**

It cannot understand clothing.

That becomes one of the show's longest-running jokes.

Humans keep demanding:

> "GIVE ME MY PANTS BACK."

> **PANTS: REDUNDANT EXTERNAL MEMBRANE. REMOVED.**

😂

But this isn't sexual nudity. Cartoon censorship can make the slime itself strategically cover everyone, which actually makes the joke better. The organism becomes humanity's clothing while insisting clothing is unnecessary.

# The deeper mystery

Here's where I think the concept can escape being merely a parody and become its own universe.

Nobody knows why the organism came.

Three possibilities remain alive throughout the series:

**Invasion hypothesis:** It is deliberately converting Earth.

**Survival hypothesis:** The meteor organism was effectively a seed or refugee and is simply doing whatever allows it to survive.

**Symbiosis hypothesis:** This is how the species reproduces across worlds. It doesn't conquer ecosystems. It **merges with them.**

And then the disturbing fourth possibility:

Earth isn't its first planet.

Somewhere out there could be civilizations where biological organisms and alien gelatinous infrastructure became completely inseparable millions of years ago.

Which means humanity's question:

> **“How do we stop the invasion?”**

might eventually become:

> **“At what point does this stop being an invasion?”**

And THAT is a fantastic spine for an absurd adult cartoon.

Because underneath all the meteor goo, missing pants, Pentagon catastrophes and people arguing with sentient Jell-O, you've got a surprisingly serious question:

**If an alien organism changes humanity, but humanity changes the alien just as profoundly, which species actually conquered which?**

By season three, even the audience shouldn't be completely sure. 🛸🟣🌎
