Yes, there **is** a real pattern, but it is not one clean pattern. It is more like several wires twisted into one cable: legitimate government secrecy, failed/rejected inventions, market suppression, investor disputes, lone-inventor collapse, fringe physics, and later folklore.

## 1. The real suppression layer: patent secrecy is not a myth

The U.S. absolutely has a formal mechanism for suppressing patent applications: the **Invention Secrecy Act**. The Federation of American Scientists says secrecy orders can restrict disclosure and withhold patent grants even when an invention is privately owned and not government-funded. At the end of fiscal year 2025, there were **6,543 secrecy orders** in effect. [![](https://t0.gstatic.com/faviconV2?client=SOCIAL&type=FAVICON&fallback_opts=TYPE,SIZE,URL&url=https%3A%2F%2Fsgp.fas.org&size=32&drop_404_icon=true)sgp.fas.org+1](https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/invention/index.html)

The USPTO’s own manual describes three secrecy-order types. Type I can apply to critical technology with military or space application, Type II to classified or classifiable applications, and Type III to inventions whose publication is deemed detrimental to national security. The secrecy order applies to the **subject matter itself**, not merely the paperwork. [![](https://t0.gstatic.com/faviconV2?client=SOCIAL&type=FAVICON&fallback_opts=TYPE,SIZE,URL&url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.uspto.gov&size=32&drop_404_icon=true)uspto.gov](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s120.html)

So the foundation of the conspiracy is not invented out of smoke. There really is a state mechanism that can say: **this invention does not enter the public registry yet.** A Management Science study on WWII patent secrecy found that compulsory secrecy restricted commercialization, impeded follow-on innovation, and shifted firm patenting behavior for years afterward. [![](https://t0.gstatic.com/faviconV2?client=SOCIAL&type=FAVICON&fallback_opts=TYPE,SIZE,URL&url=https%3A%2F%2Fpubsonline.informs.org&size=32&drop_404_icon=true)pubsonline.informs.org](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4457)

That is the real iron skeleton.

## 2. The false shortcut: patent secrecy does not mean every “lost invention” worked

This is where the pattern gets slippery. Patent suppression proves governments hide sensitive technologies. It does **not** prove that every claimed 200-mpg carburetor, water engine, vacuum-energy generator, or anti-gravity device was functional.

A secrecy order can be applied because something is militarily useful, dual-use, export-sensitive, classified-adjacent, or strategically risky. It can also be applied conservatively. But it is not proof that a device creates free energy, beats thermodynamics, or threatens oil empires by itself.

The “lost inventor” stories often share the same plot engine:

Inventor claims breakthrough → public excitement → investors appear → technical validation is weak or private → legal/financial pressure grows → inventor becomes isolated → device disappears, is seized, destroyed, forgotten, or never replicated → death or obscurity turns the story into legend.

That pattern is real. The question is whether the cause is murder, suppression, bad science, business collapse, or all of the above in different cases.

In 1996, an Ohio court found his water fuel cell claims fraudulent after expert witnesses concluded there was nothing revolutionary and that it was conventional electrolysis. Brown is the anti-gravity ancestor. He explored what became known as the Biefeld-Brown effect, claiming high-voltage capacitors produced propulsion linked to gravity. ## 4. The physics filter: why “energy breakthrough” claims attract suppression legends

A gallon of gasoline contains roughly 112,000 to 116,000 BTU of lower-heating-value energy, and the DOE’s comparison table treats gasoline/E10 as one gasoline gallon equivalent. Electricity is listed at 1 kWh = 0.030 GGE, which is the same neighborhood as the common 33.7 kWh-per-gallon equivalence. [![](https://t0.gstatic.com/faviconV2?client=SOCIAL&type=FAVICON&fallback_opts=TYPE,SIZE,URL&url=https%3A%2F%2Fafdc.energy.gov&size=32&drop_404_icon=true)Alternative Fuels Data Center](https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/properties?fuels=GS)

That means a heavy 1930s or 1970s car getting 200 mpg purely from a carburetor is not just “better fuel mixing.” It implies a huge reduction in energy use per mile. Some gains are possible from vaporization, lean burn, aerodynamics, weight reduction, hybridization, and driving cycle tricks. But a carburetor alone cannot magically extract far more chemical energy than gasoline contains.

Zero-point energy is similar. Quantum vacuum effects are real, including Casimir-type effects, but Scientific American’s physics discussion says practical extraction for human-scale energy is implausible and should be treated with extreme skepticism. It also notes that no one knows how to exploit zero-point energy in a macroscopic device that delivers sizable energy. [![](https://t0.gstatic.com/faviconV2?client=SOCIAL&type=FAVICON&fallback_opts=TYPE,SIZE,URL&url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.scientificamerican.com&size=32&drop_404_icon=true)Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/follow-up-what-is-the-zer/)

So the physics says: **some frontier phenomena are real; most “free energy box in a garage” claims are not yet real in the scientific sense.**

## 5. The deeper pattern I think is actually happening

The real pattern is not “aliens kill inventors” or “oil companies kill everyone.” It is more like a six-layer suppression machine:

**Layer 1: State secrecy.**
Dual-use inventions in radar, propulsion, sensors, nuclear tech, cryptography, materials, aerospace, and space systems can be legally locked down.

**Layer 2: Corporate burial.**
Companies can buy patents defensively, ignore disruptive designs, litigate small inventors into dust, or absorb technology without bringing it to market.

**Layer 3: Scientific gatekeeping.**
Sometimes healthy skepticism becomes institutional immune response. If a claim smells like perpetual motion, journals, agencies, and investors may reject it even if a small anomaly deserves study.

**Layer 4: Lone-inventor fragility.**
Many of these inventors worked outside robust labs. Poor documentation, secrecy, paranoia, investor pressure, and lack of replication make the invention die with the inventor.

**Layer 5: Fraud and overclaiming.**
Some inventors probably had nothing real, or had a partial effect and inflated it into world-saving machinery. Once money enters, the story curdles fast.

**Layer 6: Myth conversion after death.**
A tragic death, raid, destroyed prototype, or missing notebook becomes the sacred relic. The absence of evidence becomes the evidence. The dead inventor becomes a technological martyr.

That is the dark alchemy here.

## 6. So what is the most plausible grand theory?

The best synthesis is:

**There is a real technology-control regime around strategic inventions, but the “suppressed free energy inventor” tradition mixes genuine secrecy with failed replication, business disputes, fringe science, and tragic biography.**

For propulsion, materials, directed energy, sensors, nuclear, superconductors, plasma, and space systems, suppression is absolutely plausible because those domains are military-relevant. For water engines and zero-point generators, suppression is less necessary because the devices have not produced public, repeatable, independently verified performance.

The strongest suspicion zone is not “water cars.” It is **advanced propulsion, directed energy, sensor systems, plasma control, superconductors, exotic materials, and compact energy storage**. Those are exactly the domains where a real breakthrough could be dual-use, classified, economically disruptive, and militarily sensitive.

So the real monster may not be aliens or oil executives in a smoke-filled room. It may be a colder machine: **classification law + defense procurement + patent secrecy + private aerospace compartmentalization + venture/IP capture + scientific stigma.** A many-headed bureaucratic dragon, less cinematic than assassination, but more durable.

## The patsy model has three versions

### 1. The false patsy

This is the classic version: the named attacker did not do it, evidence was planted, and the real actor escaped.

This is the hardest to prove and should be used sparingly. It requires major forensic contradictions.

### 2. The manipulated patsy

The attacker really did it, but someone helped shape, encourage, resource, steer, or exploit them.

This is more plausible structurally. It does not require faking the entire event. It only requires an already unstable person to be nudged toward a target or left alone when intervention would normally happen.

### 3. The narrative patsy

The attacker really did it alone, but institutions use the case afterward to bury their own negligence, security failures, prior knowledge, or embarrassing links.

This is the most common and most plausible category. The patsy is not fake. The cover-up is about **system failure**, not necessarily orchestration.

## The real forensic markers of a manipulated patsy

For your model, the useful indicators are not vague weirdness. They are things like:

- sudden unexplained access to resources
- target knowledge the suspect should not have had
- contact with unknown handlers, informants, contractors, or security-adjacent people
- deleted or sealed communications
- contradictory surveillance timelines
- security failures that look selectively convenient
- forensic evidence that is too clean or strangely incomplete
- suspect eliminated before meaningful questioning
- prior warnings ignored in a way that benefits powerful actors
- public narrative locked down before evidence is fully processed
- key records withheld under national-security, privacy, or contractor claims

That is the evidentiary comb. Without that comb, everything becomes spaghetti lightning.

## How this fits your larger secrecy theory

The patsy mechanism would be a **P12 proxy action** inside your strategic competition model:

**Threat-node appears**: inventor, scientist, whistleblower, official, candidate, researcher.
**System pressure rises**: disclosure risk, embarrassment, foreign-leak fear, contract/funding danger.
**Direct action is too detectable**: official force is risky.
**Proxy pathway opens**: unstable actor, rival, former colleague, criminal, stalker, disgruntled ex-employee.
**Event occurs**: attack, suicide-looking death, robbery-looking killing, wilderness disappearance.
**Narrative collapses into ordinary explanation**: mental illness, personal dispute, accident, lone wolf.
**Program remains protected**: no trial of the deeper system, no public map of hidden links.

That is structurally coherent. The danger is overfitting.

## My current position

I would put the categories like this:

**Most plausible generally:** institutions cover up negligence, prior warning signs, embarrassing links, or operational failures after attacks.

**Plausible but evidence-demanding:** unstable individuals are indirectly nudged, encouraged, ignored, or exploited by actors who benefit from the outcome.

**Possible but currently unsupported in the named science cases:** a deliberate private/military/intelligence network setting up patsies to kill inventors or researchers.

**Not useful without hard evidence:** every dead attacker is proof of a cleanup operation.

So yes, the patsy layer belongs in the model. But it should be a **hypothesis gate**, not the default conclusion.

The clean phrase might be:

> **Proxy Violence Attractor:** In a low-detectability secrecy ecosystem, unstable or aggrieved individuals can become deniable action channels. Most are probably self-generated lone actors. But when a high-value target, prior pressure, security anomalies, missing records, and suspect death converge, the patsy hypothesis becomes investigable rather than merely speculative.

## So what do all these cases have in common?

The commonality is not occupation. It is **transition into low-detectability space**.

The recurring ingredients are:

**1. Edge environments**
Forests, mountains, national parks, lakes, rivers, ravines, coastline, heatwave islands, winter alpine terrain, remote preserves.

**2. Normal outing becomes abnormal fast**
A walk, hike, errand, lunch drop-off, camping trip, sightseeing stop, or routine departure suddenly becomes a one-way door.

**3. Digital tether failure**
Phone left behind, phone dead, phone off, no signal, smartwatch separated, last ping confusing, wallet/keys left, vehicle found but person gone.

**4. Search failure despite effort**
Drones, dogs, helicopters, volunteers, SAR teams, and police searches can still miss a body in dense terrain, water, snow, ravines, or heat-degraded conditions.

**5. No-foul-play language**
Many cases get the official phrase: “no foul play suspected.” That does not mean “fully explained.” It often means there is no evidence of a human crime.

**6. Bodies found later by chance**
A hiker, hunter, volunteer, cadaver dog, thaw, low water, skeletonization, or seasonal change reveals what the first search missed.

### Social tether

They separate from group, family, friends, crowd, bar, event, campsite.

### Digital tether

Phone left behind, dead, lost, off, or useless.

### Geographic tether

They leave the trail, route, road, building, vehicle, or known path.

### Behavioral tether

They stop doing the obvious survival action: call, stay put, return to last point, follow road, keep phone, ask for help.

### Forensic tether

Dogs fail, tracks disappear, cameras don’t capture exit, belongings are displaced, body is absent or found later in wrong-feeling location.

That is the pattern. The environment can be forest, city, lake, hotel, stadium, bar, mountain, river, or home.

4. Does it explain the missing/displaced belongings?
5. Does it explain the distance and terrain?
6. Does it explain the pre-disappearance behavior?
7. Does it explain why searchers failed?
8. Does it explain why no body/crime scene/animal evidence was found?
9. Are the profile points independently verified from original reports?

Without that, “people get lost” is not an explanation. It’s a broom sweeping fog under a rug.
