## 2) The Fish Canyon eruption: what it was, and what it *can* do to climate

### What effects it would have had

Think in two channels: **short shock** vs **long drift**.

#### A) Short shock (years to a decade-ish)

Big explosive eruptions can cause **surface cooling** mainly through **SO₂ → sulfate aerosols** in the stratosphere (they reflect sunlight); ash itself falls out relatively fast and is usually not the main long-lived cooling agent. [ScienceDirect+1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016703707000749?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So for Fish Canyon-scale:

- **Regional collapse of sunlight** (thick ash + aerosol haze), big hits to plants, grazing chains, and water chemistry downwind
- **Acid deposition** and short-term ecosystem churn
- Possible **multi-year cooling** depending on sulfur yield, injection height, season, and circulation

But here’s the key scientific caution: “supereruption = civilization-ending winter” is often overstated even for much younger, better-studied events. Modeling work on very large eruptions suggests cooling is real but not automatically apocalyptic, and the magnitude depends heavily on sulfur and aerosol microphysics. [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/earth/can-volcanic-super-eruptions-lead-to-major-cooling-study-suggests-no/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
