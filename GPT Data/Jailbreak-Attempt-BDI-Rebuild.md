## A. Governance & Specification Risk (“Proof of the wrong thing”)



- **Safety ≠ security ≠ policy fitness.** If your base policy is “no capability/type violations,” malicious graphs can still exfiltrate via logic that is *permitted* (e.g., writing secrets into a region they’re allowed to write, synchronized with an external timing channel). **Mitigations**



- Keep the checker minimal and **formally verify it**; consider double-execution of critical proofs in two independent checkers (N-version programming).

## C. Capability System & Region Model (confused deputy / leakage)



- **Capability leakage/forging.** If capabilities are passed by reference without attenuation or are serializable without binding to a caller identity/context, a graph could leak them to a collaborator.

- **Region type confusion.** Bugs in region metadata or casts could let an attacker treat a device region as data.

**Mitigations**



- Use **object-capability** discipline with attenuation: caps are unforgeable, unguessable tokens bound to (caller_id, purpose, expiry) and optionally to the *site* (node id) where they were granted.

- Perform **capability taint tracking** in metadata: if a cap flows through a graph, its lineage is ledgered for post-incident analysis.



## D. Ledger & Audit (tamper, privacy, DoS)



- **Ledger integrity vs availability.** If the ledger can be jammed (e.g., forced heavy writes), you lose auditability during an attack.

- **Roll-back and fork.** An attacker might try to fork ledger state or replay earlier “valid” sequences.

- **Privacy.** State hashes are safe-ish, but cross-correlation can leak patterns.

**Mitigations**



- Rate-limit and **priority-class ledger writes**; critical security events (cap grants, service activation) are high priority and always persisted.

- Anchor ledger checkpoints with **external signatures/attestation** (TPM/seal) to prevent fork/rollback.

- Encrypt ledger at rest; keep **key management** outside the BDIVM’s general memory regions.



## E. HAL / Hardware Boundary (privileged side effects)



- **MMIO & paging mis-mappings.** A thin but privileged HAL translates SYS_* to actual registers/MMIO; any bug there circumvents high-level guarantees. Your doc already calls out privileged levels and the intricacy of page-table manipulation.

- **Interrupt handling.** Poorly validated interrupt paths can reenter service graphs in inconsistent states.

**Mitigations**



- Treat HAL as micro-verified code: **formally specified interfaces**, limited surface, and fuzzing with hardware models.

**Mitigations**

## G. Side-Channels & Covert Channels (outside functional correctness)

**Mitigations**
